Second project of my 60 solutions in 30 days challenge: PricePilot, a pricing calculator.

THE PROBLEM

Anyone selling through a delivery app, a marketplace or a card machine is charged two different kinds of thing, and mixing them up is what quietly eats a margin.

A cost is an amount per unit. Ingredients, materials, the share of the rent that this one sale has to carry.

A rate is a percentage of the sale price. The card fee, the marketplace commission, a tax on revenue.

A rate cannot be added to the cost before the price is known, because it is a percentage of the answer. Mark up by a round number instead and you have no way to tell where the floor is, meaning the price below which you are paying to make the sale.

THE SOLUTION

One screen. Costs and rates on the left, the price and what is left of it on the right, recalculated as you type. Below that, a discount slider that says immediately whether the sale still makes money, and the same cost structure priced at three different margins.

Everything runs in the browser. No account, no server, no data leaving the device.

HOW IT IS BUILT

Money is an integer number of cents, end to end. No float ever holds an amount.

Prices round up to the cent, never down. Rounding down returns a price that delivers less margin than the one that was asked for, and that is the single direction a pricing tool must not err in.

The engine is pure: input in, result or null out. An unsolvable input, rates summing to 100% for instance, is a typed problem with its own explanation rather than an error on screen or a NaN.

WHAT WAS ACTUALLY HARD

The Vite scaffold shipped with strict turned off in the tsconfig. I wrote and reviewed the whole engine under a compiler that was barely checking anything. Turning it on afterwards surfaced real errors.

Three accessibility defects that only a browser found, with TypeScript, the linter and 60 unit tests all green: a definition list whose term sat two wrappers deep, a horizontal scroll region unreachable by keyboard at 390px, and a hint failing contrast at 80% opacity.

Lesson: unit tests and types do not replace running the thing in a browser. So the verification became a committed script, running axe-core at four viewport widths every time.

VERIFIED

77 tests passing, TypeScript strict clean, build clean, zero axe-core violations (WCAG 2.1 A + AA) at 1440, 768, 390 and 320px. The same figures hold against the live deployment, not only a local build.

Not verified: real iOS and Android hardware, and screen reader software. It is a demonstration tool: it does not model any specific tax regime and does not replace accounting advice.

Stack: React 19, TypeScript, Vite, Tailwind CSS v4, Vitest, Testing Library, Playwright + axe-core.
